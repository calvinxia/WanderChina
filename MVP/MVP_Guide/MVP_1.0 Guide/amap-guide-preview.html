a Chinese phone number.

Most guides say you need a +86 number to use AMap. You don't — not for the map. But what you give up without an account is bigger than anyone writes down, and it isn't the language.

Short version

Install AMap, skip the sign-in, switch the language to English in settings. Directions, metro and buses all work with no account and no Chinese number. What you lose is search depth — small businesses often won't appear — and every language except English stays locked.

What works with no account

Install AMap Global, dismiss the sign-in prompt, open settings and change the language to English. That's it. No +86 number, no registration, no Chinese ID.

Walking, metro, bus and driving directions all work. If all you want is to get from your hotel to a station, you can stop here — the app is usable straight out of the box.

What you give up without an account

This is the part that isn't written down anywhere, and it matters more than the language question.

Search gets noticeably thinner

POI coverage drops. Smaller businesses, independent shops and restaurants frequently don't appear in search results at all. If you're navigating between landmarks and stations you won't notice. If you're trying to find a specific tea shop somebody recommended, you will.

Location accuracy is also worse than when signed in.

Only English is available

AMap's language settings list English alongside several other languages. Without an account, English is the only one you can select — the others are greyed out, and tapping them returns a message saying the system doesn't support it yet.

So if you're French, Spanish, Japanese or Korean speaking, the recent "AMap now supports your language" coverage doesn't currently apply to you in practice.

Where foreign visitors hit the wall

AMap's own announcements describe one-tap sign-in with mobile numbers from 65 countries and regions. In practice, registration with a non-Chinese number frequently fails.

We tried an Australian mobile number. At the point of requesting an SMS verification code, the app returned: currently doesn't support from this country. The number was never sent a code.

This isn't unique to Australia — UK and Russian users have reported the same barrier. Some report signing in with email or via Alipay instead of SMS. We have not verified those routes ourselves, so treat them as leads rather than solutions.

Why this happens, most likely: account features in Chinese apps are tied to real-name verification, which is built around domestic mobile numbers. That's a regulatory constraint rather than a missing feature — which is why it has persisted across versions and is unlikely to be fixed quickly.

Translating the interface isn't translating the map

The menus and buttons translate. A lot of what sits underneath them does not.

  • Place names are inconsistent — some translated, some transliterated, some left in Chinese
  • Reviews, ratings and user photos are almost entirely in Chinese
  • Other users have reported the map jumping back to a default location abroad while viewing a Chinese city. We have not reproduced this.

For getting from A to B this is fine. For deciding whether B is worth going to, an English interface doesn't help much — that judgement lives in the Chinese reviews.

What we'd actually recommend

If youDo this
Use an iPhoneKeep Apple Maps as your default. Its mainland China data comes from AMap and it renders natively in your system language. For basic navigation it is the easiest option and costs nothing.
Need buses or metroUse AMap without an account. Transit routing is better than Apple Maps.
Are booking taxisBook through Alipay or WeChat rather than fighting AMap's login.
Haven't flown yetSave your hotel's Chinese name and address to your phone. This solves more problems on arrival than any app does.

How this was tested

  • iPhone, AMap version 16.23.1, installed from the US App Store
  • Fresh install — the app was uninstalled and reinstalled before testing
  • Three states tested: no account, Australian number registration, and signed in with a mainland Chinese number
  • Tested 21 August 2026

Android may behave differently; we only tested iOS. If your experience differs — especially if you have successfully registered a foreign number — tell us and we'll update this page and credit the correction.
